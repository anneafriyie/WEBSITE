Every piece starts with a cast of your own hairline.

Slaystrand began as a way to get luxury-house construction — hand-tied wefts, matched density, a part that actually disappears — without the luxury-house price. Here's how the studio works, and who's behind it.

studio photo — hairline cast in progress
How it started

A fitting-room problem, solved with better construction.

Our founder spent years buying units two sizes too big because nothing sat flat at her part, then paying premium-brand prices to fix it. The units that did fit right belonged to a small handful of houses charging for the name as much as the craft.

So the studio started with the part of the process everyone else skips: a physical cast of the hairline and a swatch match against the client's natural hair, taken before a single weft is cut. Everything about the fit follows from that first hour.

— Ama, founder & master weaver

Fit before anything

No standard sizing. Every base is built off your cast, so the front edge sits the way your own hairline grows.

Priced to wear weekly

We cut out the brand markup, not the construction. Same hand-tied wefts, without the four-figure tag.

Built to outlast the trend

Human hair, double-wefted, finished to hold color and pattern through repeated washing — not a single season.

Four visits, one piece.

The same sequence for every client, whether it's a first unit or a fifth.

i.

Cast

We take a mold of your hairline so the base follows your actual growth pattern.

ii.

Match

A swatch of your natural hair sets the color, texture, and density target.

iii.

Construct

Wefts are hand-tied to the cast in-studio, not cut from a pre-made template.

iv.

Fit

A final session to trim the hairline and confirm it sits flat at the part.
